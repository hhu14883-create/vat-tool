What is VAT?

Value Added Tax (VAT) is a consumption tax collected at each stage of the supply chain. Businesses registered for VAT charge VAT on sales (output VAT), reclaim VAT on business purchases (input VAT), and pay the net difference to the tax authority.

For e-commerce, the practical question is usually: which country's VAT applies, who must register, and which rate band applies to this SKU? The EU sets framework rules; each country sets rates and most compliance details.

How to calculate VAT-inclusive prices

Use the same base your storefront uses (gross vs net pricing).

Add VAT to a net price (ex VAT):

Gross = Net × (1 + VAT% / 100)

Example: EUR 100 net at 21% → 100 × 1.21 = EUR 121 gross.

Extract VAT from a gross price (VAT included):

Net = Gross / (1 + VAT% / 100)

VAT amount = Gross − Net

Example: EUR 121 gross at 21% → net 121 / 1.21 = 100, VAT EUR 21.

Marketplaces may display VAT differently (VAT inclusive checkout vs business invoices). Always align your product feed, invoices, and OSS return to the same logic.

Registration & distance-selling thresholds (seller view)

Two different ideas are often mixed: where VAT is due vs whether you need a local VAT number.

Seller FAQ

What is the difference between OSS and IOSS?

Union OSS lets you report and pay VAT on eligible B2C sales of goods and services to consumers across the EU from one registration. IOSS is only for import VAT on low-value consignments (not exceeding EUR 150) when sold B2C from outside the EU.

Which VAT rate do I show at checkout?

For B2C where your supply is taxed in the buyer's country, use that country's rate for the product category (standard vs reduced).

Does Amazon (or another marketplace) pay my VAT for me?

Under marketplace facilitator / deemed supplier rules, the platform may collect VAT on certain B2C sales. You remain responsible for correct data.
